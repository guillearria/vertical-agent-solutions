You paid somewhere between $150 and $400 for that lead. Google Ads benchmarks for remodelers land in that range, and shared-lead marketplaces look cheaper per lead but cost $250 to $500 per booked job once close rates are factored in. Then the call comes in at 2:40 p.m. while your one office person is on with a cabinet rep, and it rings out.

Below is what the whole stack costs, priced line by line, and how many booked consultations it takes to pay for itself. **Every dollar figure here is illustrative.** Your call volume, average job size, and close rate move all of it.

## The job you're actually buying

An AI voice agent answers on the first ring, around the clock. It takes the caller's name, address, project type, and timeline, offers real slots from your calendar, and texts a confirmation. It reschedules. It follows up on the web form that came in at 11 p.m.

Speed matters more than polish. The MIT / InsideSales.com lead response study of more than 15,000 leads found that contacting a lead within five minutes made it 21 times more likely to qualify than waiting 30 minutes. An agent that picks up instantly is doing the one thing voicemail cannot.

What it should not do: scope work, price change orders, or answer "so roughly what does a kitchen run?" Any number it says out loud becomes an anchor you spend the first 20 minutes of the consultation walking back. The [roofer's green-light, red-line checklist](/blog/a-roofers-green-light-red-line-checklist-for-ai/) applies to remodeling almost unchanged.

## Every line on the invoice (illustrative monthly)

**Voice agent platform: $80 to $300.** Packaged AI receptionists sit in this range. Goodcall, as one published example, runs $79 to $249 per month depending on plan, billed by unique callers, with roughly $0.50 per caller over the cap. Usage-based developer platforms (Vapi, Retell, Bland) advertise $0.05 to $0.07 per minute, but all-in with speech-to-text, the language model, voices, and telephony, budget closer to $0.11 to $0.15 per minute. At 250 calls a month averaging two minutes, that is about $60 to $75 of usage on top of platform fees.

**Telephony: $5 to $25.** A Twilio US local number runs $1.15 per month with inbound calls at $0.0085 per minute. Recording adds a fraction of a cent per minute to capture and again to store. Toll-free inbound costs more, around $0.022 per minute.

**CRM integration: $20 to $100.** JobTread and JobNimbus both have Zapier integrations, and JobNimbus can create a contact straight from a Zap. Buildertrend offers an API and Zapier connectivity. A paid Zapier plan covers most of what you need. Custom fields or anything past the standard actions means direct API work at a developer's hourly rate.

**Setup: $1,500 to $5,000 one-time, or 15 to 25 of your own hours.** Writing the call flow, listing your service area, recording the greeting, wiring the calendar, testing 30 sample calls. Spread over a year: $125 to $420 a month.

**Human review: $200 to $350.** Budget 20 to 30 minutes a day for someone to read transcripts, fix bad bookings, and call back anything flagged. At a loaded $28 an hour, roughly $250 a month. Skip this line and the model falls apart.

**Illustrative all-in: $450 to $900 a month in year one**, or $5,400 to $10,800 a year. Same shape as the [HVAC pricing breakdown](/blog/ai-answering-service-pricing-for-hvac-shops-the/).

## Break-even, counted in consultations

Say a design-build kitchen averages $60,000 and your gross margin is 30%. That is $18,000 of gross profit per signed job. If you close 30% of the in-home consultations you actually hold, one held consultation is worth about $5,400 in expected gross profit.

At $9,000 a year all-in, the agent breaks even at **fewer than two extra held consultations per year**. Two. Not two a month.

Which is why the number worth watching is not cost. It is quality.

## The math when it books tire-kickers

Run it the other direction. An estimator drives 40 minutes each way, spends 75 minutes on site, then writes it up: call it three hours at a loaded $70 an hour, plus vehicle and fuel. Roughly $250 per wasted appointment.

If the agent books 10 consultations a month and four are renters, out-of-area callers, insurance-scope shoppers, or homeowners whose budget is a third of your minimum, you have burned $1,000 in estimator time. In April it stings more, because that junk appointment sat in a slot a real project wanted.

The fixes are cheap:

- Confirm the property address and that the caller owns the home.
- Ask project type and target start window, then hard-stop anything outside your service radius.
- State your project minimum out loud ("our kitchen projects typically start around $X") and let people self-select out. That is a gate, not a quote.
- Require that all decision-makers can attend, and say so.
- Send insurance claims, rentals, and "just need a quick price" callers to a human callback instead of the calendar.

Track two numbers monthly: consultations booked, and consultations your estimator judged qualified on arrival. If the second is under 70%, tighten the script before you spend another dollar.

## Your last 90 days of call records will settle this

Export the call detail records from your phone system. Count the inbound calls that rang out or hit voicemail, and how many of those numbers never called back. Multiply by your cost per lead. That one figure tells you whether an AI phone agent is a real purchase or just an interesting article you read on a Monday.
