Every figure below is illustrative. It shows how to do the math, not what any vendor will quote you. Get real numbers from vendors and replace these before you take anything to your board.

## The museum in this example

Picture a historic house with grounds and a small nature trail. It gets about 40,000 visitors a year. Two or three people staff the front desk at any time, and the same people sell admission, ring up the gift shop and answer the phone. When a school bus pulls in, the phone rings and nobody picks up.

The museum's calls swing with the seasons. This illustrative year assumes:

- **Peak (April to August):** about 900 calls a month, from field-trip planning, summer visitors and wedding inquiries
- **Shoulder (March, September, October):** about 400 a month
- **Winter (November to February):** about 200 a month

That adds up to roughly 6,500 calls a year. At about 2.5 minutes each, it comes to around 16,000 minutes.

## What the agent itself costs

Vendors usually price an AI phone agent one of two ways, and the seasons matter a lot here.

**Per-minute pricing.** One 2026 roundup puts all-in rates at roughly $0.10 to $0.31 a minute. It also warns that the advertised rate often understates what you actually pay. At an illustrative $0.20, this museum pays about **$3,250 a year**. Its May bill would run around $450 and its January bill around $100.

**Flat monthly plans.** Published plans in the same guides mostly fall between about $49 and $599 a month. Some vendors cap minutes, others cap unique callers or calls. If a plan is sized for May, you pay May prices in January. A $300 plan comes to **$3,600 a year**, whether or not anyone calls in February.

A seasonal place should ask each vendor whether it can change plans each month without a penalty. The same logic is covered in [why a marina should price its AI phone agent by the year](/blog/why-a-marina-should-price-its-ai-phone-agent-by/).

## Phone line, setup and staff time

- **Phone line:** Twilio's published US rates are $1.15 a month for a local number and under a penny a minute for inbound calls. That comes to about $150 a year at this volume, and many agent vendors include it in their price. Budget **$150 to $250**.
- **Setup:** Writing the call scripts, loading your hours, prices and directions, and testing. Budget an illustrative **$500 to $2,500** if a vendor or consultant does it.
- **Your staff time:** Writing down the answers your front desk gives from memory, such as bus parking, stroller rules, the rain plan and what "accessible" means at a house built in 1840. Plan on **25 to 40 hours**.

## Connecting to ticketing and membership software

This is where budgets get away from people.

- **Blackbaud Altru** and **Tessitura** are built for cultural organizations. They cover ticketing, membership, fundraising and point-of-sale in one system. Neither publishes its pricing, and connecting an outside tool often takes custom work. One museum posted on Blackbaud's community forum looking for developers to help connect online group-tour booking to Altru.
- **CiviCRM** is open source and costs nothing to license, though you still pay for hosting. Reviewers say setup and connections usually need outside technical help.

An illustrative read-only connection, where the agent can check open field-trip dates or confirm a membership is current, might cost **$1,500 to $6,000** one time, plus upkeep. The cheaper route is no connection at all. The agent collects the request and creates a task, and staff confirm it in the system later.

| | No software connection | Connected |
|---|---|---|
| Agent fees | $3,250 | $3,600 |
| Phone line | $150 | $250 |
| Setup | $1,000 | $2,500 |
| Software connection and upkeep | $0 | $6,200 |
| Staff time (about $22/hour) | $550 | $880 |

| **Year two onward** | **about $3,400** | **about $5,050** |

## Break-even, counted in bookings

Illustrative profit per booking, after educator time, staffing and cleaning:

- **School field trip** (50 students): about $300
- **Adult group tour** (25 people, plus gift shop sales): about $350
- **Family membership renewal:** about $85
- **Facility rental** (a garden wedding or corporate evening): about $1,800

To cover the no-connection option's roughly $4,950 in year one, the agent has to win back bookings that would otherwise be lost to unanswered calls. That could be about 17 field trips, 58 renewals or three rentals. A mixed year works too: six field trips, two group tours, 15 renewals and one rental comes to about $5,575.

Whether that is realistic depends on one number you can measure today: how many calls go unanswered during spring field-trip season, and how many of those callers never call back. The connected option takes about two and a half times as many bookings to pay off in year one. It makes sense mainly if your staff spend real hours retyping requests into the system.

## Calls that always go to a person

Set these up as transfers or urgent callbacks before launch, whatever they do to the math:

- **Accessibility requests.** Wheelchair routes, sensory-friendly visits, ASL interpreters, service animals. The agent can share what's published, but a staff member should confirm what the building can actually do.
- **Donor and bequest conversations.** Anyone mentioning a gift, their will or a memorial goes to the director or development lead. The [30-day nonprofit plan](/blog/a-30-day-plan-for-nonprofits-that-want-an-ai-agent/) uses the same rule for donor email.
- **Artifact donation offers.** "I have my grandfather's Civil War letters" needs a curator. The agent should never seem to accept an object or give an opinion on what it's worth.
- **Lost-child and safety calls.** A missing child on the trail, an injury or a threat goes straight to staff on site with no menu. If staff don't answer, the caller is told to call 911.

## Before the spring booking rush

Over the next quiet month, have whoever is at the desk keep a tally sheet by the phone. They mark each missed call, each field-trip inquiry and each rental question. Then get quotes from two or three AI phone vendors and ask each the same things: Can we change plans by season? Can you show sample call logs? What does connecting to our ticketing system really cost, and who maintains it? With your own tally next to their prices, the break-even math above takes about ten minutes to redo.
