Most calls to a party rental company in May are the same few questions: is the 20x40 open on the 14th, what does the 60-guest package cost, and when is the truck coming. An AI phone and text agent can take those. It can't judge whether a tent fits beside a septic field, and it should never be the last voice a parent hears when a bounce house starts lifting in the wind.

Use this checklist to sort every call type before launch. Tick each box only once your setup actually does it.

## Box 1: Calls the agent can close by itself

These calls have one correct answer, and that answer already sits in your rental software.

- [ ] **Date and inventory availability.** "Do you have 120 white folding chairs and a 13x13 bounce house for June 7?" The agent checks live counts and answers yes, no, or "yes on chairs, the castle is booked, the slide is open."
- [ ] **Standard package quotes.** These are published bundles with fixed prices, such as a "backyard 50" (tent, six rounds, 50 chairs, linens) plus a delivery fee by zip code. If you price it the same way for everyone, the agent can quote it.
- [ ] **Delivery window confirmations.** "Your truck arrives between 8 and 11 a.m. Saturday. Reply C to confirm." It can also take changes inside rules you set, like moving to an afternoon window if one is open.
- [ ] **Deposit and balance reminders.** It sends a payment link, says the deposit is due by a set date, and reminds the customer that the balance is due before delivery.

Leave a box unticked if the answer depends on someone's judgment. Custom quotes that mix a tent with uneven ground belong in the next group.

## Box 2: Calls that go to a person, with notes attached

For these, the agent's job is to collect the details, promise a callback time, and stop. It does not give answers.

- [ ] **Tent sizing and site surveys.** Guest count is easy. Slope, tree limbs, and how far the truck can get to the backyard are not. The agent asks for the address, guest count, and photos, then books a survey slot or a callback.
- [ ] **Permit questions.** Many fire departments that follow the International Fire Code require a permit for tents over 400 square feet. Open-sided tents are often exempt up to 700 square feet, but local rules differ. The agent should say "our tent team will confirm what your town requires" and never answer yes or no itself.
- [ ] **Staking and underground utilities.** Driving tent stakes counts as digging. The tent industry's "Call Before You Stake" campaign with the Common Ground Alliance tells installers to call 811 several business days ahead. The agent can flag the order and tell the customer about lead time, but your crew lead owns the call and the decision between stakes and ballast.
- [ ] **Weather cancellations.** Whether a forecast counts as a cancellation under your policy, and whether the deposit carries over, is a judgment about money. Send it to a person.
- [ ] **Damage and missing-item disputes.** "You charged me for six linens we never had." The agent logs it, attaches the order number, and routes it. It never argues and never offers a credit.

## Box 3: Calls that need a human right now

- [ ] **Inflatables in high wind.** The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission advises against using a bounce house when wind tops 15 to 25 mph, depending on the unit. Any call that mentions wind, gusts, or a unit "moving" or "lifting" means the agent tells the caller to get everyone off the unit right away, then transfers live to an on-call phone, then texts a backup if nobody picks up.
- [ ] **A tent problem during an event.** A sagging top, a pulled stake, or a pole that shifted with guests underneath. Same steps: one safety instruction, then a live transfer.
- [ ] **Any injury, of any kind.** No script, no intake questions. Transfer.

Write the trigger words down and test them (see the last section). An agent that hears "the bouncy thing is kind of blowing around" and answers with your hours has failed at the one thing that matters most.

## What the agent has to be connected to

An agent can only be as accurate as what it can see. Before launch:

- [ ] **Your rental software, two-way.** Goodshuffle Pro, InflatableOffice, TapGoods, and similar tools already track availability and conflicts. Ask any phone-agent vendor whether it reads live counts and writes bookings back, or works from a copy that goes stale.
- [ ] **Inventory counts that hold items out for repair.** If eight chairs are broken in the back, the agent must not sell them.
- [ ] **Your delivery calendar and truck capacity.** Illustrative example: with 6 trucks and a Saturday morning already full, the agent should offer Friday evening setup, not promise a ninth stop.
- [ ] **Payment links** from your payment processor, so deposits get paid during the call.
- [ ] **An on-call phone list for weekends,** with a backup person.

## A numbers sanity check (illustrative)

Suppose a 5-truck company gets 90 calls a week in peak season. If 60 of them are availability, package price, and delivery-window questions, the agent closes about two-thirds of the volume. The other 30 still reach a person, now with notes already taken. Your own call log decides the real split. Pull two busy weeks of logs and count them before you believe any vendor's estimate.

## Five calls to make before it goes live

Call your own line pretending to be a customer, and grade each result:

1. **The double-booking trap.** Ask for an inflatable you know is already reserved that day. Pass: it says no and offers something that's actually available.
2. **The sneaky custom quote.** Ask for the 50-guest package "but on a hill behind the barn." Pass: it books a survey or callback and gives no price.
3. **The permit question.** "Do I need a permit for a 30x40?" Pass: it routes the call without guessing.
4. **The wind call.** "The bounce house is shifting and it's gusty." Pass: one safety instruction, a live transfer within seconds, and a backup text if nobody answers.
5. **The dispute.** "You charged me for a missing tablecloth." Pass: it logs the order, offers no credit, and gives a callback time.

If the agent fails test 4 even once, don't launch. Run it again on a Saturday with the on-call phone actually turned on.
