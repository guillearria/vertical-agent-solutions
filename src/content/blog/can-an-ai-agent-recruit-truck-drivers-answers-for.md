A driver fills out four applications on a Tuesday night. Whoever calls back first usually gets the orientation seat. If your recruiter is also your safety manager and your backup dispatcher, "first" is often Thursday afternoon. Meanwhile, annual turnover at large truckload carriers has run around 90% for years, and the American Trucking Associations is clear that this is mostly drivers moving between carriers, not leaving the industry. Those drivers are in play, and they are calling somebody.

Below are the questions small asset-based carriers actually ask about putting an AI agent on that phone. (If you want the basic definition of an agent first, we covered it in [The Agentic Wave, Explained](/blog/the-agentic-wave-is-not-just-for-tech/).)

## Can it really call and text an applicant within minutes?

Yes. That is the part of this that works well. An application hits your recruiting system, the agent calls the number on it, and if nobody picks up it leaves a voicemail and follows with a text offering two callback windows. Applications that come in at 9 p.m. get worked at 9 p.m.

Speed is the whole product here. Everything else the agent does is secondary to being the first voice the driver hears.

## What can it pre-screen for?

The factual, checkable stuff on your own qualification checklist:

- CDL class and state of issue, expiration date
- Endorsements (hazmat, tanker, doubles/triples) and whether the TSA threat assessment is current
- Months of verifiable Class A experience in the last three years, and equipment type
- Accidents, DOT recordables, and moving violations as the driver reports them
- Gaps in employment longer than 30 days
- Home location versus your lanes, and whether they want local, regional, or over-the-road

The agent collects answers and writes them into the applicant record. It does not decide who is qualified. Treat its output as a filled-in form, not a verdict.

## Can it book the orientation slot?

Yes, if orientation is a fixed schedule with seat limits. The agent reads open seats, offers the next two dates, holds the seat, texts the address and what to bring, then sends reminders 48 and 12 hours out. Confirmed seats that go unconfirmed get one more call before you release them.

## Will it chase DQ file paperwork?

This is the least glamorous and highest-value job on the list. Under 49 CFR 391.23, you have to investigate the last three years of employment, previous employers have 30 days to respond, and you must document your good-faith efforts when they don't. That documentation trail is exactly what an agent is good at: re-sending the MVR and PSP authorization links, calling a previous employer's HR line on day 7, day 14, and day 25, logging every attempt with a timestamp, and flagging the file for you when the clock runs out.

## Where are the hard red lines?

Some of these are legal, some are just self-preservation.

**Never the Clearinghouse.** A pre-employment full query requires the driver's electronic consent inside the FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse, run by you or a C/TPA you have designated. An AI agent has no role in it and should never discuss the result.

**Never pay, settlement, or bonus terms.** An agent quoting cents per mile, sign-on structure, or an owner-operator settlement sheet is creating a promise you will have to honor or explain away.

**Never the consent step on its own.** A PSP report is a consumer report under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. It requires written authorization on a standalone disclosure, and if you deny an applicant based on a report, the FCRA adverse action process applies. The agent can send the link and confirm the signature landed. A human owns the decision and the notice.

**Never live operational calls.** Hours-of-service questions, breakdowns, accidents, detention, and "I'm sitting at a shipper and nobody will load me" go straight to a human dispatcher. Same idea as the [check-call agent for a freight brokerage](/blog/what-an-ai-check-call-agent-costs-a-5-person/): routine status, yes; anything happening on the shoulder of I-80, no.

## Do we need consent to call and text with an AI voice?

Assume yes and build for it. In February 2024 the FCC ruled that AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the TCPA, so AI calls sit under the same consent rules as prerecorded ones. Put a clear line on your application authorizing calls and texts to the number provided, honor STOP immediately, and process opt-outs within the FCC's window. Ask your screening vendor or attorney to review the language once; the rules have been moving.

## What does the first 30 days look like?

- **Week 1:** Write the screening script from your existing DQ checklist. List the five things that disqualify an applicant today. Record your own orientation logistics answers.
- **Week 2:** Connect the agent to your recruiting system (Tenstreet, which now owns DriverReach, is the common one at this size) so calls write back to the applicant record. Run it in listen-only mode on real applications.
- **Week 3:** Turn on outbound calling for new web applications only. Cap it at that lane. Review every transcript daily.
- **Week 4:** Add paperwork chasing and orientation reminders. Compare time-to-first-contact and seats filled against your last two orientation classes.

## Where it falls apart

Drivers ghost regardless. Faster contact raises your show rate; it does not fix a class where the pay package is not competitive.

The nastiest failure mode is the owner-operator conversation. An agent trained on company-driver language will happily walk a lease-purchase or owner-operator prospect through the wrong screening path, or worse, imply an employment relationship you do not intend. Route those to a human at the first mention of "my truck."

Expect the agent to mishear an endorsement or a violation date. Every screened answer needs human confirmation before it drives a hiring decision. And if you are trying to size the labor savings before committing, the math in [our staffing agency breakdown](/blog/where-a-staffing-agencys-recruiter-hours-go-and/) transfers directly to a recruiter's week.

## A useful test this week

Pull your last 20 applications and write down two numbers: how many minutes passed before a human spoke to each one, and how many you never reached at all. That second number is the pool the agent is competing for. If it is under three or four, your callbacks are fine and your problem is somewhere else.
