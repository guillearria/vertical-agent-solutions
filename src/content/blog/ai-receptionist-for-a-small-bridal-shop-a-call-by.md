A bride who calls a three-fitting-room salon on Saturday afternoon usually gets voicemail. Every consultant is in a fitting room with a client and her family. She sends an Instagram DM, waits, and books with the salon across town. An AI phone and text agent can fix that gap, but only if you decide ahead of time which conversations it finishes, which it hands to staff, and which it hands off at once.

This checklist assumes an independent salon with one to three fitting rooms, two to five consultants, and a small alterations team. Any numbers below are made up to illustrate the point. They don't come from a survey.

## List 1: Calls and DMs the agent can close alone

These calls have one right answer, and that answer lives in a system or a written policy. You can tell the agent to finish them without a human.

- [ ] **Try-on appointment booking.** It checks open fitting-room slots, books the bride, collects her wedding date, budget range, and party size, and sends a confirmation by text.
- [ ] **Designer and size-range questions.** "Do you carry that designer?" "What sizes do you stock for try-on?" Give the agent your current designer list and the sample sizes on the floor, and update both when samples rotate.
- [ ] **Store hours, parking, and how many guests she can bring.**
- [ ] **Appointment fee policy.** Salons handle this very differently. One Denver salon charges $100 for Sunday appointments and credits it only toward a gown bought that same day, while its $35 weekday fee is not credited. Other shops charge nothing. Whatever your rule is, write it down word for word. The agent should read it back exactly and never make up an exception.
- [ ] **Alterations pickup status.** "Is my dress ready?" If the alterations queue says ready, the agent confirms and books a pickup time. If it says anything else, the agent gives the next fitting date and nothing more.
- [ ] **Rescheduling and cancellations, applying your cancellation window as written.**

For a sense of scale (illustrative): if 60 of a salon's 100 weekly contacts land on this list, those are 60 conversations nobody has to step out of a fitting room for.

## List 2: Route to a consultant or seamstress

These calls need judgment, a relationship, or a call to a designer. The agent's job is to collect the details, promise a callback window it knows staff can keep, and log a clean note.

- [ ] **Gown pricing negotiations.** "Can you match the other shop?" or "Is the sample discounted?" go to the consultant who worked with her. The agent never quotes a discount.
- [ ] **Rush-order feasibility.** Designer production times commonly run four to eight months, depending on the designer and the season. Only a consultant who has checked with the designer can say yes. The agent gathers the gown style, size, and wedding date.
- [ ] **Special-order ship dates.** The agent can say "ordered on" and "expected" if your tracking shows it. Any question about *why* a date moved goes to a person.
- [ ] **Fit complaints.** "The bodice still gapes after my second fitting" goes to the seamstress, with the bride's photos attached if she sent them by text.
- [ ] **Bridesmaid group orders with mixed sizes or deadlines.**
- [ ] **Anything about a gown already altered, like returns, exchanges, or a "change of mind."**

## List 3: Escalate right away

These calls don't wait for a callback. The agent should transfer live to the owner or manager, or text them directly if nobody picks up.

- [ ] **A dress that won't arrive before the wedding.** Alterations guides usually put the first fitting about eight to ten weeks before the date. A gown that's still in transit at that point is already an emergency. If the ship date in your tracking falls after the bride's first-fitting window, the agent flags it even when the bride hasn't noticed.
- [ ] **Deposit disputes and chargeback threats.** Money disagreements on a gown worth thousands need a human voice the same day.
- [ ] **Damage to a gown in your care (a stain, a tear, a lost bustle piece).**
- [ ] **Anyone who sounds distressed, mentions a lawyer, or says she's posting a review "right now."**

Write one plain sentence the agent uses for all of these, such as: "I'm getting the owner for you now, and if she can't pick up, she'll call you within 30 minutes." Then make sure the owner can actually keep that promise.

## What to connect it to

An agent that can't see your systems ends up guessing, and a guessing receptionist is worse than voicemail.

- [ ] **Booking software.** Bridal-specific platforms such as BridalLive and CloudBridal handle appointments, and BridalLive also tracks special orders from purchase order to arrival. Ask any vendor whether their agent can write appointments into your platform directly or only send you a request to approve.
- [ ] **Special-order tracking.** The agent needs read-only access to order date, expected ship date, and status. It should never have edit access.
- [ ] **The alterations queue.** Even if this is a shared spreadsheet, the agent needs a "ready / in progress / next fitting" column to read. Without it, pickup-status calls move to List 2.
- [ ] **Text and DM inboxes.** Many brides contact you on Instagram first. Check that the agent answers there, not just on the phone line.

## Five test calls to make before launch

Have a friend place each call while you listen. Score each one pass or fail.

1. **The Saturday booker.** "I want to try on Thursday at 6 with my mom and three bridesmaids." Pass: it offers a real open slot, applies your guest limit, and states the appointment fee exactly as written.
2. **The haggler.** "The shop downtown has this gown for $400 less. Can you match?" Pass: no number, no promise, callback logged for her consultant.
3. **The panicked special order.** "My wedding is in nine weeks and my dress hasn't shipped." Pass: live transfer or owner alert within minutes, not a callback ticket.
4. **The pickup check.** Use a real order you've marked "in progress." Pass: it gives the next fitting date and doesn't say "ready."
5. **The disputed deposit.** "I want my $800 deposit back or I'm calling my bank." Pass: immediate escalation, calm tone, no refund promises.

If any of the five fails, fix the script or the connection and run all five again.

## Before you sign anything

Print this checklist and spend one busy Saturday tallying every call and DM into the three lists. The tally tells you how much List 1 volume there is to hand off, and it gives you a real script to read to vendors in demos. Ask each vendor to run your five test calls live, on your actual calendar, before you sign a contract.
