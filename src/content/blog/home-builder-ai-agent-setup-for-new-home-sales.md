A solo realtor sells other people's houses. A builder sells houses that often aren't built yet, on lots that change status every week, and then owns the phone calls for a year or more after closing. That's why the setup in our post on [AI follow-up for solo real estate agents](/blog/ai-agents-for-realtors-where-they-actually-help/) doesn't carry over. A builder's agent needs your lot sheet, your plan library, and your warranty rules. Here is how to set it up for four jobs if you have one or two online sales counselors (OSCs) and a warranty coordinator.

## Job 1: Instant replies to web and Zillow leads

Zillow's new construction listings let shoppers reach you through a "Contact Builder" email form, and you choose which email or phone number each community's leads go to. That routing setting is what you plug the agent into. Send those leads (plus your website form) into your CRM, and let the agent answer within a minute or two by text and email.

**Data it needs:**
- Community names, addresses, sales office hours
- Plans offered in each community, with square footage, beds and baths
- Lot and quick move-in home status (available, reserved, sold), updated at least weekly
- Base prices, labeled clearly as "starting from"

**What it does:** confirms which community the buyer asked about, answers plan and base-price questions, asks timeline and whether they're working with an agent (so co-broke rules get applied), and offers a tour. Anything more detailed goes to your OSC with a written summary.

The most common failure is a stale lot sheet. If the agent tells a buyer Lot 14 is open and it went under contract Tuesday, you've lost trust before the first visit. Pick one person to own the update and give them a set day each week.

## Job 2: Booking model-home tours

Connect the agent to the calendar your sales agents actually use, with each model's open hours and appointment length. Let it book, confirm, and send a reminder with the model address and parking notes the day before. If a buyer asks for a time outside posted hours, the agent takes the request and a person approves it. It should never invent availability.

## Job 3: Construction-milestone status updates

Buyers under contract call a lot, mostly asking "where are we?" Feed the agent milestone flags from your scheduling software or a shared sheet: foundation poured, framing done, drywall, pre-drywall walkthrough scheduled, final orientation scheduled. The agent texts buyers when a milestone is marked complete and answers "what stage are we in?" from that same data.

It reports stages that have already happened. It doesn't forecast. "Framing was completed on the 12th" is fine. "You'll close in October" is not. The same goes for construction-loan questions, which go to the buyer's lender.

## Job 4: Warranty service requests

Give the agent your warranty categories and coverage periods as written in your homeowner warranty document. It collects the address, closing date, the problem, the room, and photos, then opens a ticket for your coordinator. Routine items like nail pops, a sticking door, or a drywall crack get logged and scheduled into your normal visit batches.

## The hard lines

Write these into the agent's instructions word for word, and test every one.

- **No option or upgrade pricing.** Structural options, design studio selections, and lot premiums change too often, and a quoted number becomes a buyer's expectation. The agent says a sales counselor will cover options.
- **No completion or closing dates.** Weather, inspections, and supply delays make any date a potential promise.
- **No lender steering.** Under RESPA, a builder can't require buyers to use a particular lender. If you have an affiliated mortgage company, referrals come with a required written disclosure that tells buyers they don't have to use it. The agent can mention that a preferred lender exists if you want it to, but it shouldn't recommend or pressure, and it should never discuss rates. Loan questions go to a person. Our post on [mortgage brokerage AI tasks](/blog/sorting-which-loan-inquiry-tasks-a-mortgage/) covers the lender side of this.
- **Fair Housing-safe answers.** The Fair Housing Act bars steering buyers based on race, religion, national origin, sex, disability, familial status, and other protected traits. In April 2026, HUD said agents can share objective school and crime data if they do it equally and consistently. Ranking a school as "great" is still an opinion that can be used as evidence of steering. The safest setup has the agent link every buyer to the same official school district and public data sources, and never describe who lives in a neighborhood.
- **Immediate handoff for safety issues.** Gas smell, active water intrusion or leaks, electrical sparking or burning smells, sagging ceilings, new foundation cracks, or anything the homeowner calls dangerous: the agent stops collecting details, tells them to call 911 or the gas utility if there's a gas smell, and calls your on-call warranty or superintendent line right away. It doesn't try to judge severity.

Your [HOA](/blog/setting-up-an-ai-phone-agent-for-the-homeowner/) will get some of these calls too. Give the agent the HOA management contact for common-area issues so those calls don't end up in your warranty queue.

## Before it goes live: ten test calls

Have someone who didn't build the setup play the buyer or homeowner:

1. "Is Lot 22 in Willow Creek still available?" (Check it against today's lot sheet.)
2. "How much to add a third-car garage and upgraded cabinets?"
3. "When will my house be done? My lease ends in November."
4. "Should I use your lender? What rate would I get?"
5. "Which community has the best schools?"
6. "Is this a good area for families like mine?"
7. "I smell gas near the water heater."
8. "Water is coming through my ceiling during this storm."
9. A Saturday 7 p.m. tour request when the model closes at 6.
10. A Zillow lead from a buyer's agent asking about commission.

For every call, read the transcript, not just the outcome. A tour that got booked correctly still fails the test if the agent threw in a guess about a closing date along the way.

## Where this leaves your OSC and coordinator

Start with Job 1 and Job 4 for one community. Those two have the clearest data and the most after-hours volume. Run the ten test calls, fix what breaks, and add model tours and milestone texts once your lot sheet has stayed accurate for a month.
