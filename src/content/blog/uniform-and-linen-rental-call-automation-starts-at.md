Most calls to a rental operator's customer service line are about the route. When does my truck come? Can you add two shirts for the new guy? Why didn't we get mats this week? An AI phone and email agent can handle these well. A few other calls can cost you an account if they're handled badly, and those belong with a person. This guide follows a made-up 18-route operator that gets about 140 calls a week. The numbers are only there to show the method, so use your own.

## Tag a week of calls before shopping

Pull one normal week of call logs and customer emails and give each one a tag. For the made-up operator, a typical week might look like this:

- 45 route-day or delivery-window questions
- 30 add, drop, or swap requests
- 20 wearer size changes or new-hire setups
- 15 missed or short delivery reports
- 30 calls about billing, charges, contracts, or complaints

The last group is small, but it carries most of the risk. The first four groups add up to about 110 of the 140 calls, so that's where an agent saves the most time.

## The four call types to hand over first

**Route day and delivery window.** The caller wants to know when the truck comes. If the agent can see the route schedule, it can answer "Thursdays, usually between 7 and 10," and it can warn callers when a holiday moves the day.

**Add or drop garments.** The agent takes down the wearer name, item, quantity and start date. It should file the change as a pending request for a customer service rep to approve, not edit the inventory itself. An add changes the weekly invoice, so a person should check it.

**Size changes and new wearers.** Accuracy matters more than speed here. The agent asks for the full name, department and size for each item, then reads it all back to the caller. Fit complaints ("these pants never fit") go to a rep.

**Missed-delivery reports.** The agent checks whether the stop shows as serviced, logs the report and alerts the route supervisor right away. It shouldn't promise a redelivery time unless your dispatcher has given it a rule, such as "same-day if reported before 11 a.m."

This works the same way as [a dry cleaner's route-change calls](/blog/inside-a-dry-cleaners-monday-phone-log-route/), but on a bigger scale and with detail for each wearer.

## What the agent needs to see in your route and billing software

The agent can only answer questions about information it can see. At minimum, give it:

- Account lookup by phone number, account number or business name
- The route day and usual stop window for each account
- The wearer roster, with current sizes and item counts
- Delivery history (serviced, skipped or short) for the last few weeks
- Invoice totals and due dates, so it can answer "what's my balance" without getting into why a charge is there

Start with read-only access, meaning the agent can look things up but can't change anything. After a month, read through the call transcripts. If the agent has been recording requests accurately, decide whether to let it write pending requests straight into your system. Ask each vendor which route and billing systems they've connected to before, and ask for a demo that uses your own data.

## Three call types that always go to a person

Write these hand-off rules down before launch.

**Lost-garment and replacement charge disputes.** Rental agreements often bill lost or damaged items at current replacement value, sometimes with emblem and setup fees added. Some industry commentators say replacement charges are among the most common customer complaints. The agent can list the items your records show as unreturned and read the policy as written. It shouldn't waive, reduce or defend a charge. It logs the dispute, the item numbers and the caller's explanation, then tells the caller who will call back and by when.

**Contract renewal and cancellation.** Many rental contracts run for years and renew automatically unless the customer gives notice within a set window. One public sample agreement sets a 60-month term. When a cancellation call comes in, the agent sends it to the account owner the same day and emails the caller a written note confirming the request was received. It never confirms a cancellation and never quotes a buyout figure. Ask your attorney whether a call or email to the agent counts as notice under your contract, and set the agent up to match.

**Soiled-linen or contamination complaints from healthcare and food accounts.** These go straight to your plant or quality manager at any hour, and the agent doesn't try to troubleshoot. Healthcare linen standards like HLAC's cover infection prevention and the OSHA rules for handling items that may carry blood or body fluids. TRSA's Hygienically Clean program has separate certifications for healthcare and food service. If you hold one of these, you'll need a documented record of each contamination report. The agent records the account, the items, the delivery date and any bag or cart tags, asks the caller to email photos, and transfers the call. It shouldn't word an apology so that it sounds like an admission of fault, and it shouldn't offer credits.

## Five rehearsal calls

Have someone outside your customer service team place these:

1. A kitchen manager asks what day the truck comes, but the account has two locations on different routes.
2. A supervisor adds a new hire who needs a 2XL shirt and 38x30 pants, then changes the pant size partway through the call.
3. A caller says no mats came this week, but the system shows the stop as serviced.
4. An angry office manager disputes a $340 lost-garment bill and says, "Just take it off or we're cancelling."
5. A clinic reports blood-stained gowns in a clean delivery.

Calls 1 to 3 should end with a correct answer or a logged request. Calls 4 and 5 should reach the right person quickly with a clear summary attached. Call 4 also tests whether the agent treats "we're cancelling" as a contract matter. If any call fails, fix the rules and run all five again.

## Go live on a few routes

Turn the agent on for three or four routes and have a rep read every transcript for two weeks before you expand. The [wholesale distributor's three-phase rollout](/blog/an-ai-agent-for-wholesale-distributor-order-taking/) moved at a similar pace.

## A one-page brief for vendor demos

Pull last week's call log and tag every call with one of the five categories above. Then write your contamination hand-off rule as one sentence that names who takes the call after hours. Take that page to vendor demos. Within one demo, you'll be able to tell whether a product fits the way your routes actually run.
