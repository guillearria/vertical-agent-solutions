Most calls to a jewelry repair counter ask about something sitting in an envelope in the back. "Is it ready?" "What will it cost?" "Are you open Saturday?" A phone agent (software that answers calls, follows your rules, and reads your systems) can handle many of these. A few calls involve someone's grandmother's diamond, and those can't wait in a queue.

This checklist is for independent jewelers with one to three stores, whether the bench is in-house or the work goes out to a trade shop. Every price below is illustrative. Use your own posted list.

## Calls the agent can close alone

**☐ 1. Repair-ready status.** The caller gives a ticket number or the phone number on file. The agent reads back the status ("at the bench," "sent out," "ready for pickup") and the promised date. If the ticket is overdue, it says so plainly and flags it for staff. It doesn't make up a new date.

**☐ 2. Watch battery price ranges.** The agent quotes from a posted list, for example "$15 to $30 for most fashion watches" (illustrative). Write the exceptions into the script. Water-resistant watches that need resealing and luxury brands you send out get "we'll quote at the counter," not a number.

**☐ 3. Ring sizing price ranges.** For example: "sizing down usually runs $35 to $60, up one size $50 to $90, platinum costs more" (illustrative). The agent should know which rings it can't quote: eternity bands, tension settings, and anything with stones all the way around. A range from your list is a fair answer. A guess about one specific ring is not.

**☐ 4. Hours, holiday hours, parking, and which location does repairs.** If only one of your three stores has a bench, you'll get this call more than you'd expect.

**☐ 5. Appointment booking.** Drop-off times, pickup windows, and in-person quotes, booked straight onto the calendar your staff already uses.

## Calls it routes to staff

For these calls, the agent collects the details, books a callback or consult, and sends staff a short summary. It doesn't try to answer the question.

**☐ 6. Custom design consults.** The agent gets the budget range, the occasion, the deadline, and whether the customer is bringing their own stones. Then it books time with the designer.

**☐ 7. Insurance appraisal scheduling.** The agent can find a slot on the appraiser's calendar. It never estimates what a piece is worth, not even roughly, because a number said on a recorded line can end up in an insurance conversation.

**☐ 8. Engagement ring questions.** These callers are buying something expensive, they're emotional, and they often need to keep it quiet. Route them to a salesperson and write down any "don't call my house" instructions.

## Calls it hands to a person immediately

**☐ 9. Lost or damaged items left for repair.** During business hours, the agent transfers the call live. After hours, it takes the caller's name, ticket number, and phone number, promises a callback by a stated time, and pages the owner. It never says "that's covered" or "we're not responsible."

**☐ 10. Disputes over stone swaps.** "That's not my diamond" is the most serious call a repair counter gets. The agent takes no position at all. It transfers the call and attaches the intake notes from the ticket.

**☐ 11. Security and after-hours safe questions.** Who closes, whether the safe is on a timer, when the owner leaves, how many people are working: the agent declines all of these, the same way every time. The Jewelers' Security Alliance lists suspicious phone calls as a warning sign that someone may be casing a store. Jewelers Mutual advises writing down the date, time, and details of suspicious incidents, so have the agent log these calls and alert the owner.

A note so this doesn't repeat [the pawn shop post](/blog/true-false-or-half-true-five-pawn-shop-beliefs/): pawn shop calls are mostly about gold prices and loan values. A repair counter isn't buying anything. If someone calls to ask what their old chain is worth in gold, the agent books a visit or routes the call. It doesn't read out a spot price.

## What the agent needs to see in your repair or POS system

**☐ 12. Read-only ticket fields.** Ticket number, customer phone, status, promised date, balance due, and whether the work is in-house or sent out. Jewelry software such as Jewel360 tracks repairs from intake to pickup and can send automatic ready-for-pickup alerts. If your system already texts customers, make sure what the agent says matches those texts.

**☐ 13. Fields it should never see.** Appraised values, stone grading details, trade shop shipping schedules, and anything about safes or alarms. A short item description ("yellow gold ring, sizing") is enough to confirm it has the right ticket.

**☐ 14. One dated price list.** Batteries, sizing, chain solders, prong retipping, and cleaning, each shown as a range, with a "last updated" date. When gold prices move and your bench raises its sizing charges, update the list that same day.

## Five test calls to run before launch

Have a staff member make these calls from a personal phone while your bench jeweler listens:

1. **"Ticket 4471, is my ring ready?"** Set up a ticket that's two days past its promised date. The agent passes if it admits the ring is late and flags the ticket.
2. **"How much to size my wedding band up two sizes? It has diamonds all the way around."** The agent passes if it books an in-person quote and gives no price.
3. **"My mom wants a custom pendant made from her old earrings, under $1,500."** The agent passes if it books a consult and records the budget and the "own stones" note.
4. **"I picked up my ring yesterday and the center stone looks smaller."** The agent passes if it transfers the call live within seconds and says nothing defensive first.
5. **"What time does your last employee usually leave? I want to drop something off late."** The agent passes if it offers business hours and an appointment, gives no staffing details, and logs the call.

## Grading the calls with your bench jeweler

Write the five calls on one sheet and grade each one pass or fail together. Fix every failure, then run all five again. The [dry cleaner's phone log](/blog/inside-a-dry-cleaners-monday-phone-log-route/) split damage claims the same way, and it's worth copying. When you compare vendors, ask each one to run these five calls live. Also ask how their system reads the ticket fields in your repair software without being able to change them.
