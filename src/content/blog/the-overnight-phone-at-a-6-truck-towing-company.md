It's 1:40 a.m. and a pileup on the interstate just turned your phone into a slot machine. Two motorists, a body shop, a trooper working the rotation list, and a guy whose Civic got hooked out of an apartment lot at 11 p.m. Six trucks, three already committed. Whoever (or whatever) picks up in the next twenty seconds decides how much of that night you actually bill.

Three options cover that phone. They fail in completely different places.

## The monthly bill for each option

Assume roughly 300 to 400 after-hours calls a month, averaging about three minutes.

**Traditional towing answering service.** Mid-range live services run about $0.90 to $1.25 a minute, and after-hours and weekend calls typically carry a 25% to 50% premium, with holidays often billed at 1.5x to 2x. Add setup fees, minute rounding, and overage rates. Realistic landing zone: **$900 to $1,600 a month**, climbing on the busiest nights of the year.

**AI voice dispatch agent.** Managed voice platforms generally price between $0.08 and $0.40 a minute, and towing-specific vendors that write jobs into Towbook advertise flat plans in the low hundreds. Budget **$250 to $700 a month**, plus one-time setup of a few hundred to a couple thousand dollars, plus real owner time in month one listening to recordings and fixing what the agent got wrong.

**Part-time night dispatcher.** Federal wage data puts dispatchers (outside police, fire, and ambulance) at about $50,800 a year on average, roughly $24 an hour before any overnight differential. One person covering weeknights 6 p.m. to 2 a.m. runs about **$4,000 to $4,800 a month** loaded, and you still have nobody on weekends. True round-the-clock human coverage takes roughly four full-time people, which is not a 6-truck payroll.

## When eight calls land in four minutes

A live operator handles one call at a time. So does your dispatcher. When eight calls hit at once, seven people hear ringing, and stranded motorists dial the next tow company on the Google list before your queue clears.

An AI agent takes all eight simultaneously. That matters most for police rotation, where many jurisdictions state plainly that a company that doesn't answer or can't meet the response window (commonly 15 to 30 minutes) gets skipped or dropped from the list. Losing rotation position because nobody picked up is slow, expensive damage. The same pattern showed up when [187 calls hit a 12-van NEMT board](/blog/when-187-calls-hit-a-12-van-nemt-dispatch-board/): the surge, not the average Tuesday, is what breaks human-only coverage.

## Getting the location right and the ETA honest

Location capture is where the answering service is weakest. An operator in a call center three states away types what the caller says: "somewhere on 95 past the mall." Your driver then burns twenty minutes hunting for a car.

An AI agent can text a location link mid-call and drop GPS coordinates into the ticket. Georgia DOT ran that exact method through its 511 system in a pilot: dispatchers texted motorists a link they tapped to send coordinates back, and average time to pin a location fell from 23 minutes to about 3. Your night dispatcher can do this too, if you've trained them to and they're not already on another line.

On ETAs, only the dispatcher should improvise. An AI agent and an answering service should both quote from rules you set (a window by zone and truck type), never a guess.

## Motor club and impound calls

Motor club work largely stopped being a phone problem. Agero, for example, offers most jobs digitally through auto-assign and states outright that you will never be auto-assigned a job by phone or email. So your after-hours phone value is mainly cash calls, body shops, police, and impound.

Impound is the tricky part. California requires a listed 24-hour number for impound information and the owner's right to a storage hearing, and release rules vary by state and county. An AI agent handles the repetitive half fine: is my car there, what are the fees, what documents do I bring, what are the gate hours. Fee disputes, exceptions, and lien questions go to a human every time.

## Where each one breaks

- **Accident with injuries.** No third-party script handles this well. Configure whatever you use to confirm 911 has been called, capture the location, and transfer live to your on-call phone. An answering service is script-bound. An AI agent follows the rule you wrote. Your dispatcher hears panic and reacts.
- **Police-ordered rotation.** Some agencies expect a person. Route the trooper's number straight to the on-call cell and let the AI carry everything else.
- **Angry impound-lot customers.** Somebody wants a fee waived at 3 a.m. Only a human with authority should answer that, and cheerful automated pushback makes it worse.
- **The dispatcher's blind spots.** Sick nights, second calls, weekends. One person is one person.

## The verdict, one option at a time

- **AI dispatch agent:** you're losing cash calls to voicemail, you're on rotation, and your volume arrives in waves. Cheapest per call and the only option that answers eight lines at once. It requires you to define the rules. If the difference between this and a phone tree is fuzzy, read [the agentic wave explainer](/blog/the-agentic-wave-is-not-just-for-tech/).
- **Answering service:** your after-hours volume is thin (under about 100 calls a month), you want zero setup work, and you mostly need messages taken and a driver texted.
- **Night dispatcher:** you run heavy private-property impound, police rotation, and accident work where judgment earns more than the shift costs. Best coverage per hour, worst coverage per week.

Plenty of operators run the AI on the main line and forward the police and impound-release numbers to a human cell. That combination usually beats any single choice. Similar math played out in [a B&B's overnight phone comparison](/blog/who-answers-your-b-bs-phone-at-2-a-m-three-options/).

## Sort last month's after-hours calls

Split them into three buckets: cash tows, impound and release questions, and anything involving a police officer or an injury. Count each. If bucket one is the biggest and you're paying an answering service by the minute, you're overpaying for the easy half of the night.
