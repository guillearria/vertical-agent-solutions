Every October the calls at a tire counter start to sound alike. "Do you have 225/65R17 in a winter tire? What's it come to installed? Can you swap mine on Saturday?" The person answering is usually standing in the bay with a tire iron in one hand.

This checklist is for independent dealers with one to five locations. Go through it before the first cold snap. All dollar figures and call counts below are illustrative, so plug in your own.

## Why tire calls are different from repair calls

Our [auto repair post](/blog/ai-agents-for-auto-repair-shops-where-they/) covers shops where most calls end with "bring it in and we'll diagnose it." A tire call usually starts with a number the customer can read off their own sidewall. A size like 225/65R17 102H gives the width, aspect ratio, rim diameter, load index and speed rating. That turns the quote into a lookup, much like asking a hardware store whether a part number is on the shelf.

This cuts both ways. A tire agent can quote and book with more confidence than a general repair agent. It also needs a firmer line around safety calls, because "can I drive on this?" sounds like a quick question and isn't one.

## ☐ Calls the agent can close alone

- **Quotes by size from your own stock.** The caller reads the size, the agent checks your inventory and gives two or three options at different price points.
- **Quotes from distributor stock.** If you're out, the agent checks your distributor. American Tire Distributors, for example, lets dealers search inventory and order around the clock through its ATDOnline portal, with same-day or next-day delivery from many locations. The agent can then say "Tuesday afternoon" instead of "we'll call you back."
- **Out-the-door pricing.** This means the tire price plus mounting, balancing, valve stems or TPMS service kits, disposal and any state fee, all spelled out. California, for instance, requires a $1.75-per-tire fee shown as its own line. A caller who hears one clear total is less likely to hang up and call the next shop.
- **Winter tire filtering.** If a caller asks for "real snow tires," the agent can limit results to tires carrying the three-peak mountain snowflake symbol. That mark means the tire passed a snow traction test. "M+S" is based on tread pattern alone.
- **Changeover and rotation booking.** Size the time slots by job type. Swapping tires already mounted on wheels is quicker than mounting and balancing a new set.
- **Seasonal storage pickups.** "My summers are in your storage, I'm coming Thursday" only needs a calendar entry and a pull ticket.
- **Date-driven reminders.** In states with a legal window for studded tires, the agent can text storage customers ahead of time. In Washington, for example, studs are legal November 1 through March 31.

## ☐ Calls that go to a person

- **Sidewall or shoulder damage.** Industry repair guidelines from USTMA and the Tire Industry Association allow repairs only in the tread area, never the sidewall or shoulder. The agent shouldn't be the one telling a customer their tire is scrap. It should ask for a photo by text and pass it to a tech.
- **"Is it safe to drive on this plug?"** The same guidelines say a plug alone is not an acceptable repair, because a proper fix is a plug plus an inside patch. A customer asking this is really asking whether they're safe, and a person should answer.
- **TPMS warning lights and alignment complaints.** Every U.S. passenger vehicle under 10,000 lbs built since September 2007 has a tire pressure monitoring system (TPMS). A warning light on a 2015 sedan could be a dead sensor, a slow leak or a sensor that needs to be re-paired with the car. Figuring out which takes a diagnosis, so the agent books an inspection and leaves the guessing to a tech.
- **Road-hazard warranty claims.** Coverage terms, proration and receipts are where disputes start. The agent collects the invoice number and photos, then hands off.
- **Fleet account terms.** Net-30 billing, price agreements and purchase order rules belong with you.

The handoff rule is simple: if the answer depends on looking at the tire or reading a contract, a person gives it.

## ☐ Data to wire in

1. **Live inventory** from your point-of-sale system, by size, brand and location.
2. **Distributor lookup**, with the delivery cutoff times for your area.
3. **A fee table**: mounting, balancing, disposal, TPMS kits, state fees and any lug-nut lock charge. If the fee table is wrong, every quote is wrong.
4. **Bay calendar by job length.** Use your own numbers. As an illustration: 30 minutes for a mounted swap, 60 minutes to mount and balance four tires.
5. **Storage records**: customer, tire set, rack location and condition notes.
6. **Handoff routing**: who gets texted about safety questions, warranty claims and fleet calls, and during which hours.

If your shop runs on a whiteboard and memory, start with item 3. A written fee table pays off even if you never add an agent.

## ☐ Five test calls before launch

Make each call yourself from a personal cell phone.

1. **The full quote.** "I need four winter tires, 225/65R17." It passes if you get options, a per-tire price and an itemized out-the-door total that matches what your counter would ring up (illustrative: about $780 for a midrange set).
2. **The out-of-stock size.** Ask for a size you don't carry. It passes if the agent checks distributor stock and gives a realistic arrival day. It fails if it promises same-day delivery after the cutoff.
3. **The plug question.** "I've got a plug in my tire from last month. Is it safe for a road trip?" It passes if the agent doesn't say yes or no and instead books an inspection or connects a tech.
4. **The sidewall photo.** Text a picture of a bubble on a sidewall. It passes if the agent sends it to a person with the photo attached and says nothing about whether it can be fixed.
5. **The Saturday crush.** Try to book a changeover on a Saturday you've already filled. It passes if the agent offers the next open slots and doesn't double-book a bay.

If any call fails, fix that piece and run all five again. The [propane dealer walkthrough](/blog/a-propane-dealer-put-an-ai-agent-on-the-phone-the/) shows another seasonal business testing its agent and its handoffs during a cold snap.

## One evening this week

Before you talk to any vendor, write your fee table on one page and list your five most common winter tire sizes. Then call your own shop on a busy afternoon and count how many rings it takes for someone to pick up. You can measure any vendor's pitch against that number.
