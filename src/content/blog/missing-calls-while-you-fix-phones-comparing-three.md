You have a phone open on the bench, a cracked screen half-off its adhesive, and the shop line is ringing for the fourth time in ten minutes. If you run one to four locations, the person at the bench is often the only one who can pick up. So every call costs you either a sale or focus on a delicate repair.

There are three common ways to take that load off: an AI phone and text agent, an online booking and quote widget, or a part-time counter hire. Below, each one is tested against the five calls a repair shop actually gets.

## The five calls, and who handles each

| Call type | AI phone/text agent | Booking & quote widget | Counter help |
|---|---|---|---|
| "How much for a cracked iPhone 15 screen?" | Good, if it reads your price list | Good, for callers willing to click | Good |
| "Do you have the part in stock?" | Only if it can see your inventory | Only if it's tied to inventory | Good, can walk to the shelf |
| "Is my repair done?" | Good, if it can look up the ticket | Weak, most people call anyway | Good, but pulls them off the counter |
| Walk-in hours | Excellent | Fine, if posted | Fine |
| Warranty or water-damage dispute | Should hand off | Can't handle | Depends on the person |

## Option 1: An AI phone and text agent

This is software that answers your line by voice or text, works from your price list and repair tickets, and passes anything tricky to a human.

**Where it shines:** price questions and hours calls, which tend to be the bulk of a repair shop's ringing. It can answer at 8 p.m. when someone drops their phone in a parking lot, and it can text a booking link instead of keeping the caller on the line.

**Where it breaks:**
- **Quoting what you don't stock.** If your price sheet lists "iPhone 15 screen, $189" but you're out of that panel, the agent will happily quote and book it. The fix is to have it say "price confirmed when you arrive" unless it can actually check your inventory.
- **Genuine versus aftermarket.** On an iPhone 15, Apple's Parts and Service History screen can show an "Unknown Part" message if a non-genuine display is used. A good agent should state which grade of screen the price covers, so you don't end up arguing about it at pickup.
- **Passcodes.** Set a hard rule that the agent never asks for or accepts a passcode by phone or text. People take this seriously for good reason: in 2021, reports revealed Apple had paid a multimillion-dollar settlement after technicians at a contractor's repair facility posted a customer's private photos from her phone. Passcodes get collected in person at drop-off, by you.
- **Angry callers.** Someone whose water-damaged phone died again two days after you fixed it doesn't want a script. Program the agent to recognize words like "warranty," "refund," and "still broken," then take a callback number and alert you right away.

**Illustrative cost:** Published plans run from about $49 to $300 a month. Goodcall lists plans at $79, $129 and $249, and Smith.ai's AI-only service starts at $95. Expect a few hours of setup loading your prices and rules.

## Option 2: An online booking and quote widget

Repair-shop software like RepairDesk offers a widget for your website. The customer picks their device make, model and problem, sees the quote you've set, and books a time. In the U.S. it can also collect payment when they book.

**Where it shines:** customers who would rather tap than talk, and multi-location shops, since customers can pick the nearest store.

**Where it breaks:** it does nothing about a ringing phone. Callers checking repair status, people who can't tell a 15 from a 15 Plus, and anyone upset about a repair will still call. It has the same stocking problem as the AI agent: a widget will quote a model you can't actually fix this week unless you keep it updated.

**Illustrative cost:** RepairDesk plans start around $99 per store per month (third-party listings show Essential at $99 and Growth at $149). Check whether your plan includes the appointments feature. If you already pay for repair software, the widget may cost little or nothing extra.

## Option 3: Hiring counter help

A person at the counter can walk to the parts drawer, calm down an angry customer face to face, and take a passcode properly at drop-off.

**Where it breaks:** cost and coverage. The U.S. median wage for retail salespeople was $16.62 an hour in May 2024. A 25-hour week comes to roughly $1,800 a month in wages before payroll taxes. That person also goes home at closing, takes lunch, and calls in sick. A new hire who misquotes a board-level repair can do more damage than a bot that says "let me have the tech call you."

## Which fits your shop

- **One location, owner at the bench:** start with an AI agent for prices, hours and status checks, with strict hand-off rules for disputes. Add the widget if your repair software already includes it.
- **Two locations:** use the widget and the AI agent together, with one shared price list feeding both so quotes match.
- **Three to four locations or 40+ tickets a day:** you probably need counter help at your busiest store anyway. Put the AI agent on overflow and after-hours so your hire works the counter instead of the phone.

The same principle shows up in [collision repair shops that lead with status texts](/blog/status-texts-first-phone-second-an-ai-agent/): the "is it done?" call is the cheapest one to automate and the one that eats the most of your day.

## Before you pick one

For one week, keep a tally sheet by the register. Mark each call as price, stock, status, hours or dispute. If most of the marks fall in the first four columns, an AI agent or widget will cover them. If disputes fill a column, that's a sign you need a person on the counter, or a better warranty policy. Then call two vendors, give each your actual iPhone 15 price, and ask them to show what their agent says when that part is out of stock.
